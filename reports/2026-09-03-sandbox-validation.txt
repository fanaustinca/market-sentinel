1. Why one path proves nothing
------------------------------
Seed 42 is a market built with no signal whatsoever. Here is what our
tests say about it:

  ljung_box: stat=19.9648 p=0.0296 -- autocorrelation detected within 10 lags -- not a random walk  <-- flags structure
  jarque_bera: stat=1.0692 p=0.5859 -- returns are consistent with a normal distribution
  variance_ratio_q2: stat=1.0476 p=0.0169 -- VR(2)=1.048 above 1 -- moves reinforce each other (trending)  <-- flags structure
  variance_ratio_q5: stat=1.0821 p=0.0601 -- VR(5)=1.082, indistinguishable from 1 -- consistent with a random walk
  variance_ratio_q10: stat=1.0746 p=0.2676 -- VR(10)=1.075, indistinguishable from 1 -- consistent with a random walk

Two tests report structure in a market containing none. Nothing is
wrong: at a 5% significance level, one path in twenty gets flagged by
chance. Judging the generator on a single path would be the exact error
this project exists to avoid.

2. The right question: does it reject at exactly the expected rate?
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Generating 1000 independent null markets of 1260 days each...

  expected 5%, acceptable band [2.93%, 7.07%]

  ljung_box             4.90%   PASS
  variance_ratio_q2     4.80%   PASS
  variance_ratio_q5     4.40%   PASS
  jarque_bera           3.90%   PASS

Rejections arrive at precisely the rate the mathematics predicts. Too
many would mean the generator leaks structure; too few would mean the
paths are suspiciously well behaved, which random data never is.

  The basket was chosen to be *defensible* -- names that were already
  large in 1999, including ones that went on to do badly. It still beat
  the real sector index by nearly ten points a year, and none of that
  gap is skill. It is the return to knowing, in 2026, which companies
  were still worth typing out. Every strategy number above inherits it,
  which is why the comparisons that matter are between strategies on
  the same basket, never between a basket and an index.

  READING IT

  Knowing the earnings calendar moved Sharpe +0.827 -> +0.815 (-0.012) and drawdown -18.4% -> -18.7%.

  So the forecast improvement does not become a strategy improvement.
  That is not a contradiction and it is worth being precise about why.
  Volatility targeting responds to a higher forecast by holding less,
  and an earnings move is symmetric -- the stock is as likely to gap up
  as down. Holding less across a symmetric move avoids variance and
  gives up the drift in equal measure, on 1.3% of stock-days, while
  turnover rises 2.0 -> 2.6 a year and costs real money.

  The honest summary: the earnings calendar is a genuine and very
  strong volatility signal, and position sizing is the wrong instrument
  to express it with. A signal about the *size* of a coming move pays
  where size is what is traded, not where direction is.
